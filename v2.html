04

Research philosophy

Understand the system → explain the mechanism → influence direction early → make the evidence travel.

In a complex, data-heavy system, pain points rarely exist in a single dimension; solving them usually requires a set of features working in synergy.

Lately, my research has focused on system-level investigation: reconciling different products, users, workflows, and mental models into a coherent experience rather than optimizing isolated surfaces.

Once those relationships have a shared language, the architecture starts to reveal itself: what should be unified, what should remain distinct, where structural debt has accumulated, and what changes need to move as a system.

That UX architecture blueprint is where I believe research creates its highest leverage.

A finding is only as strong as the explanation behind it. I treat the first “why” as a hypothesis, then pressure-test it against competing explanations, contradictory evidence, and variables that could change the interpretation. Years of neuroscience lab research trained me to be suspicious of convenient stories.

In a complex enterprise system, a drop-off in one workflow can point to permissions, data state, terminology, navigation, or the sequence itself. I don’t stop at “users are confused.” I trace where behavior diverges, compare the surrounding conditions, and test the competing explanations until the pattern holds across both telemetry and qualitative evidence.

Research creates the most leverage before direction hardens. I invest upstream, while assumptions and decisions are still cheap to change. Working in trust and cybersecurity sharpened my instinct for de-risking: move fast when decisions are reversible; apply more rigor when the consequences aren’t. As AI makes execution cheaper and faster, that judgment matters even more.

Producing good evidence is half the job. I build the reporting rhythms, dashboards, and programs that put it in front of the people whose decisions it should change. Research scales when evidence travels, not when everyone is asked to become a researcher.
